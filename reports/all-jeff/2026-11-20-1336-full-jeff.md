### 💬 How they talk to their audience

**Takeaway: These accounts barely reply to their own followers. Their replies are mostly link drops or short thank-yous, so the real engagement happens in the post itself.**

- @joshuatlindsey replied to about 12 comments with the same line: "Escaping toxic work? Take my Toxic to Thriving quiz and get your personalised next steps free." Those replies got 0-2 likes. It reads as a sales drop, not a conversation.
- @heyismail.co's replies are almost all promo links for a free Notion template. They get 0-2 likes. No real back-and-forth shows up in the data.
- @dr.dan.phd replies the most in substance. His longer answers got the most likes of any replies collected: 193, 196 and 352 likes on his comments about friendship and partners.
- Tone is calm and direct. Replies are 1-4 sentences. Emojis are rare, and @dr.dan.phd is the only one whose replies carry real ideas.
- Praise and criticism were not visible in the data. Comments under their posts were not collected, so we cannot see how they handle pushback.

#### Techniques to adopt

- [ ] **Answer with a real idea, not a link.** @dr.dan.phd's substantive replies (193-352 likes) beat the promo drops (0-2 likes). Why: people reward the substance.
- [ ] **Keep replies to 1-4 sentences.** Their best replies are short and direct. Why: short replies are easy to read on a phone.
- [ ] **Ask a follow-up question back.** Nobody in this set does it well, so it's open ground. Why: it turns a reply into a thread.
- [ ] **Use the reader's own words when you reply.** Why: it shows you actually read the comment.
- [ ] **Thank people for sharing a personal story, then add one useful tip.** Why: it rewards openness without pretending to be a therapist.
- [ ] **Avoid pasting links into replies.** The promo-link replies got almost no likes. Why: they look like spam to the reader.
